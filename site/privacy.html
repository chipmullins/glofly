Privacy Policy

Last updated November 8, 2026

The short version

  • Glofly keeps what it needs to run your account, your chats and your calls. Nothing more.
  • No ads, no selling or renting your information, no tracking you around the web.
  • Glofly's own apps you sign in to with Glofly, bots in constellations and game servers you link only get what Glofly tells you first, and you can cut them off any time.
  • Messages are encrypted on their way to and from our servers, but they are not end-to-end encrypted: they're stored on Glofly's servers so they reach all your devices.
  • Delete your account any time from Settings (or without the app). It's removed for good after 30 days.
  • Glofly isn't a storage service: chats and files can be cleared at any time, and we aren't responsible for lost data. Keep copies of anything important.

Who we are

Glofly is a messaging app built and run by Chip Mullins in Lexington, Kentucky, USA ("Glofly", "we", "us"). This policy covers Glofly for Windows, Mac, iPhone, iPad and Android, Glofly in your browser at chat.glofly.app, and this website. Questions about this policy or your information: support@glofly.app.

What we collect

Things you give us

  • Account details: your email address, username, display name and password. We store only a scrambled form of your password (an Argon2 hash), never the password itself.
  • Your birthday: used to check you're at least 13. It's never shown to anyone, unless you turn on Share my birthday (off unless you turn it on): then your friends see that it's your birthday on the day, never the year or your age.
  • Your profile: profile photo, header image, pronouns, "About me", your board (favorite games, a constellation you love, a note, things you're into, and whether to show what you're playing), status and custom status. Location and camera details are removed from photos and videos when you upload them.
  • Messages and files: the messages, photos, videos, files, voice messages, stickers, GIFs, polls and reactions you send, messages you schedule to send later (kept until they're sent), and the chats and groups you're in. When you make a sticker, Glofly's own server cuts out its background; your photo isn't sent anywhere else.
  • Constellations: the constellations (communities) you create or join, the nebulas (channels) in them, your roles there, the messages you post (including in trails and star charts), events you create or answer, your answers to a constellation's onboarding questions, the optional nebulas you pick, and your notification choices for it (like muting it or ignoring @everyone).
  • Two-step sign-in: if you turn it on, the codes we email you and, if you add one, the phone number codes are texted to.
  • Friends and blocks: friend requests, friends, and people you've blocked.
  • Settings: your privacy choices (read receipts, last seen, typing, who can send friend requests, who sees your games), quiet hours, whether to show glows or share your birthday, and which friends you hear about when they come online (all of them, ones you pick, or none). Appearance, sound and device choices stay on your own computer, phone or browser.
  • Messages to us: if you email us, we keep that conversation to help you.

Things created as you use Glofly

  • Sign-ins: one record per signed-in device, with a device name (like "Windows · DESKTOP-4KQ2" or "Android · Pixel 8"), when it signed in and when it was last used. You can see these and sign devices out in Settings → Account. To email you when a device you haven't used before signs in, we also keep a scrambled fingerprint of each device's name and type (not its location or network address). Glofly in a browser keeps you signed in with a cookie; it's the only cookie Glofly sets, and it isn't used for anything else.
  • Presence: whether you're online, away or busy, and when you were last seen. This lives briefly in memory and is shared only with your friends, following your settings.
  • Games: Glofly on a computer checks for games it recognises; this check happens on your computer and only the game's name is sent. If "Show what I'm playing" is on, your friends see the game while you play. Glofly also counts your playtime per game for My games on your profile, which you can show to everyone, only your friends (the starting choice) or only you, and hide games from.
  • Glows: how many days in a row you and a friend have messaged each other. They're only shown if you turn on Show glows.
  • Connected apps (Sign in with Glofly): when you sign in to one of Glofly's own apps (like Gloslate or Tablespin) with Glofly, which app it was, what you allowed it (and which constellations' roles, if you picked any), and when it last used your sign-in. The app gets sign-in tokens that Glofly keeps only in scrambled form; each lasts an hour and can be renewed for up to 60 days unless you disconnect the app.
  • Linked game servers: when you link your account to a game server, which constellation and game it's for, the player identifier the game gave you (a FiveM or RedM license identifier, or a Minecraft player ID) and the name you played under, so the server can recognise you. The 6-digit link code itself is never stored.
  • Keys you make: if you make a constellation's API key, webhook or game-server key: its name, a scrambled form of its secret, when you agreed to the Developer Terms, and 30 days of a key's activity (what it did, never what anyone wrote) so the constellation's owner can see what their integrations do.
  • Notifications: if you allow notifications, your phone or browser gives Glofly a push address so we can tell it about new messages and calls (from Google on Android, from Google and Apple on iPhone and iPad, and from your browser's maker in a browser). It's kept with that device's sign-in and deleted when you sign out or turn notifications off.
  • Calls: when a call started and ended, who was in it, and whether it was missed. We don't record calls, screen shares or Broadcast now.
  • Broadcast now: while you're live, which friends are watching (so you can see who's there). This lives only in memory and is gone when the broadcast ends.
  • Days you use Glofly: one note per day you use Glofly, saying only which kind of app you used (computer, phone or browser). It doesn't say what you did, when, or who with. We use them as totals, to see how many people use Glofly and whether new people come back, and when you ask us for help, to see which apps you've been using.
  • Error reports: when something in Glofly breaks (a crash, or a call that couldn't connect), the app sends us what went wrong, where (including which screen was open), the app version, your system and browser, and your account, so we can fix it. They never include what anyone wrote, and email addresses, long numbers and sign-in codes are removed first. Turn this off in Settings → Privacy → Send error reports.
  • Network address: your IP address is used for a few minutes to stop abuse (like too many sign-in attempts). We don't keep it in our records.

How we use it

  • To run Glofly: deliver messages and calls, show your profile to the right people, keep you signed in, and sync your devices.
  • To send account emails: confirming your email, resetting your password, sign-in codes, security notices (like "your password was changed"), and updates about your account being deleted or restored. We may also email you about important changes to Glofly, like these policies or Glofly shutting down. We don't send marketing emails.
  • To keep Glofly safe: stop spam and break-in attempts, and look into abuse that's reported to us.
  • To fix problems and improve Glofly, using error logs that leave out message contents.
  • To understand how Glofly is used, as totals only: how many people use it each day, how many calls are made, and how often features like photos, voice messages or polls are used. We count that a message was sent, never what it says.

We don't read your messages. We would look at specific information only when needed to fix a problem you asked us about, to investigate a report of abuse, or when the law requires it.

Who can see what

  • Anyone on Glofly who searches for you can see your username, display name and profile photo, unless one of you has blocked the other.
  • Your profile's extras (since 0.9.1): your header image and pronouns show to anyone who can open your profile. Your board shows to everyone, only your friends, or only you (you choose; everyone to start). The Activity tab lists games you've played or started (only if My games is shared with that person) and public or ask-to-join constellations you joined in the last 30 days, never chats, calls or private constellations; it shows to your friends to start, and you can choose everyone or only you. Mutual friends and mutual constellations only ever show what you and the other person both have (never your whole friend list), and you can limit them to friends or turn them off. Family-managed accounts only show these to their friends.
  • Your friends also see your status, "About me", when you joined, last seen, and what you're playing (all following your settings). When you go live with Broadcast now, your friends can be told you're live (you choose each time), and you see the names of friends who are watching. When you watch a friend's broadcast, they see that you're watching.
  • People in a chat with you see the messages, photos, files and voice messages sent there. If you leave a group, what you already sent stays visible to the group.
  • People in a constellation with you see your name, photo, roles and what you post there. Its owner and moderators can remove posts and members; when a moderator removes a post, the first 120 characters are kept in that constellation's moderation log, which its moderators can see.
  • Glofly's own apps you sign in to with Glofly (like Gloslate and Tablespin, run by the same person as Glofly; Sign in with Glofly isn't offered to other companies) get only what Glofly shows you before you choose Allow: always your display name, username, profile photo and a Glofly ID for your account; your email address only if you tick it that time; and your roles only in constellations you pick. Each app uses this under its own privacy policy, which Glofly links on that screen. Disconnecting an app in Settings → Connected apps signs it out, and it can't get anything more from Glofly.
  • Bots and integrations a constellation's owner adds (marked BOT or TOOL) can see its members' display names, usernames, roles and when they joined, and post where the owner allows. A webhook is told when people join or leave or their roles change, never what anyone wrote. A bridge can read messages only in the nebulas the owner picked, and Glofly shows "Messages here are also shared with …" above the message box there. These bots are run by the owner or someone they chose, under their own policies, and never get direct messages or group chats.
  • Game servers you link see your Glofly display name and username, your roles in that constellation, and whether you're allowed in (not a member, banned, held back by a safety measure, or timed out), each time you join, for as long as you stay linked.
  • Anyone, even without Glofly: a constellation's invite link shows its name, description, pictures, how many members it has and who invited you. Public constellations are listed in Glofly's constellation directory. Your personal invite link shows your display name and photo to whoever opens it, until you reset it.
  • The Glofly team, when someone reports a message or a person: we see the reported message and up to five messages before and after it (or, for a person, your recent messages with the one who reported you), plus the names involved and the reason given. A copy is kept with the report so it can be reviewed even if the messages are later edited or deleted. Reports are never shown to the person reported.

How long we keep it

  • Messages and files stay until you delete them or your account is removed, unless we clear them sooner (see "We don't promise to keep your data" below). Deleting a message removes its text, photos, files and voice messages; a small "message deleted" note stays in the chat.
  • Deleting your account (Settings → Account, or these steps if you can't use the app) hides it right away and signs you out everywhere. After 30 days your profile, photo, friends, direct messages (for both people), the messages and files you sent in groups, and the notes of days you used Glofly are removed for good. Log in within the 30 days to restore it. We email you when the deletion is set, if you restore your account, and once it's done; that last email is the final thing we send, and your address goes with the account. Apps you signed in to with Glofly and game servers you linked stop getting anything from Glofly right away, and those connections are removed with the account.
  • Reports and the copy of the messages they include are kept for up to a year after they're resolved, so repeated problems can be spotted, and may be kept longer if the law requires it.
  • Error reports are kept until the problem they describe is fixed, then deleted.
  • Connected apps and linked game servers stay until you disconnect or unlink them, the app or key is removed, or your account is removed. An app's sign-in codes expire within a minute and its tokens within 60 days. A key's activity record is kept for 30 days. What an app, bot or game server already received is kept under its own privacy policy. Bots and game servers must delete it within 30 days of your unlinking or asking, under our Developer Terms.
  • Constellations: what you posted in a constellation stays until you or its moderators delete it, or the constellation is deleted. Its moderation log is kept as long as the constellation exists.
  • Email links (password reset, confirm email) expire within 7 days and are cleared soon after. Two-step codes expire after 10 minutes.
  • Backups: our database is backed up daily for recovery after a problem. Removed information can stay in those backups for a short time until they're replaced.

We don't promise to keep your data

Glofly is a messenger, not a storage or backup service. Messages, photos, files and other content can be removed from our servers at any time, for example when we clear out older chats to keep Glofly running, during maintenance, or because of a technical problem. Backups are for keeping Glofly running, not for restoring individual chats. We aren't responsible for data that's lost or removed, so please keep your own copies of anything important.

If Glofly shuts down

Glofly can be paused or shut down at any time. If we plan to shut it down for good, we'll try to give notice ahead of time in the app, by email or on glofly.app, so you can save what matters to you and ask for a copy of your information. When Glofly shuts down, we'll delete the information we hold (backups included, as they're replaced), except anything the law requires us to keep. If Glofly ever changes hands instead, this policy keeps applying to your information.

Your choices

  • Change your profile, email and password, and see where you're signed in, in Settings.
  • Turn off read receipts, last seen, typing indicators, "Show what I'm playing", error reports, or friend requests from everyone. Choose who sees My games, your board, your activity and your mutual friends and constellations, and whether to show glows or share your birthday.
  • Turn on two-step sign-in, and set quiet hours.
  • See and disconnect apps you signed in to with Glofly (Settings → Connected apps), and unlink game servers (Settings → Linked game servers; on phones, Apps & game servers).
  • Mute a constellation, ignore its @everyone and role mentions, and change your onboarding answers and the optional nebulas you see.
  • Turn off notifications, or just message previews, on each phone.
  • Block anyone. They can't message you, call you or find you, and they aren't told.
  • Delete messages you sent, or your whole account.
  • Email support@glofly.app to ask for a copy of your information or anything else about it. We'll answer within 30 days, and send your copy as a file you can open.

Security

Connections to Glofly are encrypted (https), passwords are stored as Argon2 hashes, saved sign-ins are encrypted by your computer or phone, two-step sign-in and new-sign-in emails are available, and our database isn't reachable from the internet. No system is perfectly secure, so if something ever goes wrong that affects your information, we'll tell you.

Children

Glofly is for people 13 and older. We don't knowingly collect information from anyone younger, except for a family account: one Glofly sets up for a child under 13 only at a parent's or guardian's request and with their consent. A family account can't be found in search and can only talk to friends added at the parent's request. A parent can ask us at any time to see, change or delete their child's information, or to remove the account. If you believe a child under 13 has an account any other way, email us and we'll remove it.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date above. For important changes, we'll tell you in the app or by email before they take effect.
